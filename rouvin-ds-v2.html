First: v1 was written against the wrong product

Rouvin DS v1 assumed a logistics and fleet platform, inferred from the logo's chevron. The implementation report shows what Rouvin actually is: a vendor trading and wallet console — buy stock, hold to an unlock time, sell for profit, plus referrals, claims, commissions, deposits and withdrawals, in ₹, with UPI, PAN, Aadhar and IST. Every icon, label and pattern in v2 is rebuilt against that reality. That correction alone changes more of the interface than the restyle does.

01 — Direction

Every axis moved

v1 was a clean corporate console: navy sidebar, white cards floating on a tinted page, soft 10px corners, geometric type. v2 is a trading ledger: a dark instrument rail, tinted panels sitting into a white page, sharp 4px structure, a grotesque display face, and money set in a real tabular mono. Nothing carries over except the two brand colours, which are fixed by the logo.

02 — Foundations

Dual-mode colour

Every colour resolves through a semantic variable — --panel, --text-2, --bad — never a raw hex. Light and dark are two value sets behind the same names, which is why the toggle works and why a component written once is correct in both.

Three rules that carry over, and two that change

Carried over — the orange rule

In light mode orange fills still take dark ink, never white (3.05:1 fails both ways). Orange text on light is still #D24E00 only.

Carried over — warning is mustard

Never brand orange. Orange means “this is the action”; mustard means “look at this”. Both keep their filled glyph.

Carried over — colour is never alone

Every status carries a glyph and a word. Profit and loss also differ in sign and in position, not only in hue.

Changed — in dark mode, orange becomes readable text

#FF8038 on the dark panel is 7.23:1. Dark mode is the one place orange may set labels and figures, which is why the dark theme can lean on it more freely.

Fixed — v1 input borders were failing

v1 specified slate-300 #BEC7D8 for input borders: 1.70:1 against white. WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.11 wants 3:1 for the boundary that identifies a control. v2 uses #7A88A3 — 3.57:1 — and #5A6C90 (3.43:1) in dark.

03 — Foundations

Type: a grotesque, a humanist, and a real mono

Archivo for display — a tight industrial grotesque that sets a page title like a ticker header. Figtree for UI — warmer and rounder than Plex, and more legible in a 13px table row. JetBrains Mono is promoted from “IDs only” to a first-class face: every rupee figure, quantity, countdown and timestamp is set in it, because in a wallet product the numbers are the content and they must align in a column.

Number rules — new in v2

  • Every figure uses font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums. Columns of money must align on the decimal.
  • Currency is ₹ 48,250.00 — symbol, thin space, Indian grouping, always two decimals. Never ₹48250.
  • Signed values carry the sign: +₹ 6,140.00 / −₹ 820.00. The sign leads, before the symbol.
  • Countdowns are HH:MM:SS in mono so the digits do not jitter as they tick.
  • All timestamps say IST explicitly. Vendors reconcile against bank statements.
  • Never round a balance, a profit or a quantity to look tidier.

04 — Signature

The cut

v1's signature was the route line. v2 retires it and replaces it with the cut: a 45° slice off the top-right corner of any surface that carries a monetary value, with the notch filled in accent. It comes straight out of the logo — the R has a diagonal cut through it — and it does a job: if a panel is cut, there is money in it. Vendors learn that in one session.

No cut — this is a count, not money

Discipline: the cut appears on money panels, the wallet balance strip, purchase and sale confirmation modals, and payout rows. It never appears on navigation, on plain content cards, on counts, or on more than four surfaces in one viewport. Applied everywhere it would stop meaning anything.

05 — Components

Keycap buttons

v1's buttons were flat fills. v2's have a 1px border, a 2px shelf beneath, and they physically press down two pixels when clicked. In a product where a click spends real money, the control should feel like it was pressed. Press one.

Variants

Solid is the page's one main action — navy in light, a lighter navy in dark so it stays visible. Accent is reserved for the money-moving moment: buy, sell, claim, submit a withdrawal. One accent per screen region, never two. Quiet replaces v1's secondary. Ghost for in-row and low-priority. Danger always names the object in its confirmation.

Sizes

States

The loading label keeps the same width so the button does not jump mid-transaction. Verb consistency holds: Sell now → Sold. Claim bonus → Claimed.

Full-width, in a purchase modal

Money buttons state the amount. A vendor should never have to scroll up to check what they are about to spend.

06 — Components

Phosphor, bold — and matched to the real product

v1 used Lucide at 1.75px, and its inventory listed trucks, routes and map pins for a product with none of those. v2 moves to Phosphor at weight="bold" — visibly heavier and more geometric, which holds up better against Archivo and reads at a glance on a phone — and the inventory is rebuilt around what a vendor actually does. Active navigation and status glyphs use weight="fill".

Two icon fixes carried in from the report

The bonus popup currently pulls coin, gift and star glyphs from react-icons — that library goes, and those become Phosphor coins, gift and sparkle. The top-bar bell is decorative with no aria-label; in v2 it either becomes a real notifications control or it is removed. A bell that does nothing costs trust.

07 — Layout

Rail, context header, and a balance that never leaves

v1 spent 264px of every screen on a labelled sidebar, and put a breadcrumb, a dead bell and an account name in a 64px bar above it. v2 gives that width back to the content. Seven destinations do not need labels once the icons are learned — they need to be reachable in one click and to be obvious on a phone.

The rail — 72px, always dark

Dark in both themes, so the vendor always knows where the frame ends and the ledger begins. Active items use the filled glyph plus a 3px accent edge. Every button carries an aria-label and a tooltip on hover and on focus. Below md the rail becomes a bottom bar with the five most-used destinations — a phone-native pattern, not a hamburger drawer.

192px reclaimed on every desktop screen. On a 1366px laptop, that is 14% more room for a purchase table.

Context header — sticky

One block that replaces the breadcrumb bar and the per-page h1: page title, tab strip, and the balance strip — wallet balance and unrealised profit, on screen at all times. In v1 a vendor had to navigate to Wallet to see their balance before deciding whether they could afford a purchase. Now the answer is always in the top-right.

08 — Components

Tags, alerts, forms, tables, states

Status tags

v1 used sentence-case pill badges. v2 tags are mono, uppercase and tracked — they read as ledger annotations, and they visually separate from body copy at a glance in a dense row.

Quantity stepper

Steppers are keyboard-operable and typeable. The max is the lower of remaining quota and available stock, and the reason for the cap is stated in helper text — never a silently disabled button.

09 — Rearrangement

The dashboard, restructured

This is the change the client will feel. The v1 dashboard stacks seven blocks and reports the same money twice — six stat cards, then a four-KPI “financial snapshot” underneath. v2 collapses it to four blocks, removes the duplication, and puts the two questions a vendor actually opens the app to answer at the top: what am I worth right now, and what needs me today.

v1 — seven stacked blocks

1 · Page title
2 · Greeting row + copyable vendor ID
3 · Four shortcut tiles
4 · Six stat cards + profit banner
5 · Financial snapshot — 4 KPIs
6 · Two charts
7 · Recent activity (5)

Blocks 4 and 5 report overlapping figures. Nothing surfaces an unlocked trade, so a vendor can miss a sale window entirely.

→

v2 — four blocks, nothing repeated

1 · Position + Action queue (two columns)
2 · One unified metric strip (4 tiles)
3 · Two charts
4 · Recent activity (5) + view all

Greeting and vendor ID move into the profile menu on the rail. Shortcut tiles are replaced by the command palette and the action queue, which are contextual rather than fixed.

10 — Standards

Accessibility, in both themes

WCAG 2.2 AA is the bar, and it now has to hold twice — every pairing was measured in light and dark. The figures on the swatches above are the real measurements, not estimates.

Fixed in v2

  • Input borders now pass. v1's #BEC7D8 was 1.70:1 against white; SC 1.4.11 needs 3:1. Now 3.57:1 light, 3.43:1 dark.
  • Focus ring works on dark. Orange on the dark canvas is 6.82:1; the ring moved to 2px with a 3px offset so it reads on both.
  • The bell gets a purpose or gets removed. No unlabelled decorative controls in the tab order.
  • Bonus popup loses the bounce and honours prefers-reduced-motion. Confetti becomes a 220ms opacity and scale settle.
  • Countdowns announce politely at 10 minutes and at unlock, not on every tick.
  • Theme respects the OS on first load, then remembers the vendor's choice.

Still non-negotiable

  • Text ≥ 4.5:1 · large text and component boundaries ≥ 3:1, in both themes
  • Every rail button labelled; tooltips on hover and focus
  • Modal focus trap, Esc to close, focus returns to the trigger
  • Touch targets ≥ 44px; the mobile bottom bar uses 48px
  • Usable at 200% zoom; reflows at 320px with no horizontal scroll
  • Colour never alone — profit and loss carry a sign and a glyph
  • Destructive confirmations name the object and the amount
  • Greyscale test: the screen must still be fully readable with colour removed
